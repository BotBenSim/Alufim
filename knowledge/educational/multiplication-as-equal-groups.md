# Multiplication is equal groups; division is sharing them out

## Decision

The multiplication and division games teach the *meaning* of the operation before the
fact. Multiplication is shown as **a number of equal groups** — 4 groups of 3 apples, each
group visibly boxed — not as a memorised table entry. Division is shown as **fair sharing**
— 12 apples dealt into 3 boxes, how many does each get.

Both ride the same three-step visual scaffold as add and sub
([math-visual-scaffold](math-visual-scaffold.md)), so a parent tunes them the same way:

- `fullCount` — every group drawn in full, so the answer can be counted
- `countOn` — one group drawn, the number of groups as a numeral
- `numbers` — digits only

Division never produces a remainder. The total is generated as divisor × answer, so
sharing always comes out even.

## Why it helps the child

A child who only memorises "6 × 4 = 24" cannot tell you what to do with six bags of four
apples. Equal groups is the model that makes the fact mean something, and it is the same
picture that later carries area, fractions and ratio. Sharing is division's most intuitive
entry — children fairly share sweets long before they meet the ÷ sign, so the game names
something they can already do.

Leaving remainders out at this stage is deliberate: "3 left over" is a genuinely separate
idea, and meeting it while still learning what division *is* muddles both.

## How it works

The multiplication generator puts the larger factor first, so a question is few full groups
rather than many groups of one — easier to see and to draw. Because order doesn't change
the product, `a × b` and `b × a` share one no-repeat key, so a run won't ask the same fact
twice wearing different clothes.

Wrong answers are the neighbouring products — for 6 × 4 the choices come from 18, 30, 20
and 28, never 23 or 25. Those are the mistakes a child actually makes when their groups are
off by one, so a correct tap means something. Falling back to answer ± 1 would let a child
who has no idea eliminate the distractors on sight.

## Watch-outs

- Above roughly 5 × 5 the full-count drawing gets crowded; that is the natural point for a
  band to move to `countOn`, which is how the default bands are set.
- Division is shown only in its sharing (partitive) form. Grouping — "how many bags of 3
  can I fill from 12" — is the same arithmetic but a different question, and it isn't
  covered yet.
- Remainders, and division as the inverse of multiplication, are both unbuilt.
